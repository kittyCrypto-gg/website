# CRT cache comparison, 26 October 2026

**Result: no reliable performance improvement. Keep this experiment unmerged.** The warmed raster lattice does not remove the full-screen backdrop processing, and the measured gains and regressions do not meet the requested lag-free outcome.

## Environment and method

Chromium 153.0.8010.0, Linux, 1280 × 720 viewport, device scale 1. Graphics used **SwiftShader software rendering**: ANGLE (Google, Vulkan 1.3.0 (SwiftShader Device (Subzero) (0x0000C0DE)), SwiftShader driver-5.0.0). There was no physical GPU. These numbers do not establish performance on Jasper, integrated graphics, or a discrete GPU.

The baseline was `a74755628f19ce9e1d41d66191d3c1640f84a568`. Twelve final timing trials used three paired repetitions per profile, alternating version order and launching fresh Chromium. Both versions used the built About-page HTML, CSS, assets and actual effects code, with backend applications and the terminal removed from the fixture. Preparation completed before timing, including the same 95 text-effect targets in both all-effects variants.

Each trial measured 2.5 seconds of idle, then native mouse gestures down and up 1,800 CSS pixels at 900 pixels/second. Actual offsets were checked. The timed source was `024708f`; the final downloaded source `708792a` adds the high-DPI fallback guard, which does not change the warmed 1× path measured here.

A static phosphor-only page correctly required zero idle redraws. Its animation-callback cadence remained approximately 60 Hz. Phosphor-only scrolling also recorded zero main-thread `Paint` duration in both versions; newly exposed tiles still required some raster work. This is evidence that unchanged page painting was already being reused. The backdrop/composition work remains during scrolling.

The all-effects profile was extremely slow and variable in this software renderer. Its lower frame rates must not be interpreted as a measurement of the relative effect costs on real GPU hardware. There is no consistent overall win here.

## Fidelity and behaviour

**21 of 24 final screenshot pairs matched exactly.** All eight cached 1× pairs and all eight native-fallback 2× pairs were exact. Three native-fallback pairs at scale 1.25 differed, with maximum channel differences of 1, 1 and 3 out of 255. Strict pixel parity across the full matrix is therefore unproven. The matrix covered light/dark modes, phosphor opacity 0.048/0.12, scroll offsets 0/1,200, and device scales 1/1.25/2. All effects were enabled. Animations were paused at 400 ms, their ready promises resolved, and two animation frames elapsed before capture. An initial repeated native-baseline control at scale 1.25 matched exactly. An expanded eight-pair baseline-versus-itself control had two non-zero comparisons, each differing by 1/255 at two pixels. This establishes some renderer variability but does not fully explain the larger remaining mismatch. The raw JSON identifies every case. No claim of universal pixel equivalence is made.

An earlier high-DPI raster prototype differed by up to 12 channel levels. It was rejected. The final cache activates only at device scale 1 and uses the original SVG at other scales or fractional pattern pitches. The original filter, backdrop filter, multiply blend, opacity and animation definitions remain intact. Removing `will-change: filter` also regressed the all-effects experiment and was reverted.

The final downloaded branch passed these ten runtime checks:

- Downloaded branch prepares and activates the raster texture.
- Texture cache remains bounded to four live object URLs.
- Disabling phosphor clears the cached texture.
- Re-enabling phosphor restores a decoded cached texture.
- Fractional physical tile dimensions retain the SVG.
- Device-scale change invalidates the texture and selects fractional-scale SVG fallback.
- Returning to an integral device scale restores the cached texture.
- Canvas failure preserves the original SVG effect.
- Late image preparation cannot reactivate a disabled effect.
- Full application boot initialises its controls and the cached phosphor layer.

Both checkouts built successfully. The final downloaded branch passed type, nesting, architecture, entry, generated-shell and route checks, plus whitespace validation.

## Reproduce and inspect

See [the benchmark instructions](../crt-cache-experiment.md). [Raw measurements and summaries](crt-2026-10-26.json) contain every final trial, frame-interval statistics, dropped-frame counts, paint/raster timings, pixel comparisons and environment details. The harness also writes Chromium traces and paired PNGs locally.

For a hardware conclusion, repeat the comparison on the target machine with a visible Chromium window. This experiment caches the lattice only; it does not expose or replace the browser’s private DOM tile cache.
